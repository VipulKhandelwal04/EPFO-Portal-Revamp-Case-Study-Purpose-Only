EPFO Design Study · Comparative Analysis

Current Portal vs. Revamped Portal, screen by screen

Every pro and con below is graded against the same live prototype, not a hypothetical - the Current Portal column is a byte-faithful replica of the real EPFO Member e-Sewa site, and the Revamped columns describe exactly what exists in the redesign today, including its gaps.

Seventeen of the twenty-five screens carry real functional or informational differences. The other eight are visual-language redesigns only - that distinction is called out plainly rather than papered over.

ⓘ  Reading the columns: Pro = a real improvement demonstrated in the prototype. Con = a real, verified gap or trade-off - not a hedge. Improvement = the concrete next step that would close that specific gap, not a vague aspiration.
Sitewide, not screen-specific

Navigation

Current

Top horizontal bar, hover dropdowns, no persistent wayfinding.

Revamped

Persistent left sidebar, accordion groups, one open at a time, keyboard and screen-reader tested.

Landing page after login

Current

Static profile printout - identity, KYC status, employment record.

Revamped

Task-first dashboard (open issues, balances) with Profile split into its own screen.

Motion

Current

None - every navigation is a hard cut, matching the real site.

Revamped

Cross-fade transitions; dashboard action cards morph into their claim detail panel.

Accessibility baseline

Current

Replica of the real site's actual gaps - small tap targets, no focus management.

Revamped

WCAG-AA contrast, 44px touch targets, route-change focus announcement, focus-trapped dialog.

Sign-out & identity

Current

Name and Sign Out live only in the horizontal nav bar.

Revamped

Also pinned to the masthead, same line as the org title - visible regardless of scroll or sidebar state.

Visual language

Current

Sharp corners, solid teal bars, dense - matches the real government portal.

Revamped

Rounded cards, white panel headers, more whitespace - a modern account-UI language.

Core narrative - where the redesign's argument actually lives
Login Full sign-in page, notices, quick links for pre-login flows. By designDeliberately kept visually identical to Current, down to colour and spacing - the one screen every visitor hits before any redesign argument has been made. ConZero differentiation here on purpose - a reviewer judging only this screen sees no evidence a redesign happened at all. ImprovementDone: the screen itself stays untouched; a one-time, dismissible "What's new in the redesign" banner now appears on Home right after login (dismissal remembered for the session), so the case is made without breaking the visual-replica intent.
Home / Dashboard Doubles as the profile page - identity, KYC status, employment record, one static rule-based alert. ProTask-first dashboard: every open issue (rejected claim, stalled claim, unsigned nomination, a balance untransferred since 2019) plus the contribution-gap alert now live in one prioritized "Needs attention" feed, ranked by urgency, with the resolved alert's audit trail tucked behind a "View timeline" disclosure instead of sitting always-on as a second surface. The whole feed can also be collapsed with the same accordion pattern as the sidebar nav groups (rotating caret, animated grid-rows collapse), re-expanding automatically whenever Home is reopened so a dismissed feed can't quietly hide a new issue. PF and Pension balances are masked behind asterisks by default, with a "Show balances" toggle to reveal them, so a shoulder-surfing risk on a shared or public screen isn't always-on the way it is today. Profile summary now sits above Needs attention. ConThe contribution-gap alert is still illustrative demo data - what shows as "auto-resolved" hasn't been demonstrated against a real ECR feed, so the dashboard's proactive-detection claim is unproven against live employer data. ImprovementThe surface-merge is done; the remaining gap is real: connect the Alert Centre to EPFO's actual monthly ECR feed so contribution gaps and blank exit dates are detected from live data, not an invented timeline, before this is shown to a real member.
Profile No separate concept - this is what Home already shows. ProSplitting identity/KYC/employment out of the dashboard keeps Home focused on action, not record-keeping, and a compact Profile summary card on Home (name, UAN, a live "X of 4 verified" KYC count) now links through to the full screen, so the first-glance muscle memory isn't lost. The full screen's own KYC Summary card was previously static and out of sync with reality (Bank Account always showed a hardcoded green VERIFIED, and the Father's Name row was missing entirely) - both now read live off the same KYC state Home does, so re-verifying on the KYC screen updates Home and Profile together instead of only one of them. ConThe KYC count still tops out at 3 of 4 by design (the member's own PAN-name mismatch is never wired to a fix); the note under the count now explains that, but the PAN fix itself remains off-portal and unmodeled. ImprovementDone: a live one-line note under the Home summary card lists exactly which items are open and says the PAN name fix happens at the PAN portal, not here, so "3 of 4" no longer reads as an unresolved bug.
KYC Aadhaar / PAN / Bank table with plain VERIFIED or UNVERIFIED pills - no explanation of what a mismatch means. ProArguably the highest-value screen in the redesign: names the exact root cause (a seeded bank name the portal marks verified today, caught only by exact-match scrutiny at claim time), replaces the falsely reassuring "VERIFIED" bank pill with "FAILS CLAIM SCRUTINY," and now genuinely resolves both defects it surfaces - bank and father's name each move through a pending state to VERIFIED and correct the name on record, independently of each other. The bank fix now models the real re-seed step from the fix-guide: a form asking for the corrected account holder name plus a cancelled cheque or passbook upload, with an exact-match check that rejects any name not matching Aadhaar letter for letter, teaching the exact-match rule at the moment of entry instead of asserting it in copy. The PAN-side father's-name fix stays a one-step re-verify ("I've corrected this at the PAN portal"), matching the guide's route where the correction itself happens at NSDL, not on this portal. Claim Filing's "Fix KYC first" path also detects whichever field just cleared and stops citing it, closing the loop end to end. The father's-name check itself is new: no KYC screen, current or redesigned, has ever surfaced it before, added from real member complaints (Reddit, X), not invented for the study. ConThe member-side routing is checked against a practitioner fix-guide (kustodian.life, 2025, part of this study's research base): bank and PAN corrections are member-alone, and the employer gate applies only when EPFO's own master record is wrong, which is the Joint Declaration path Modify Basic Details already models. That source is a guide, not an EPFO circular. The Aadhaar-side gate, by contrast, is now anchored: EPFO's circular of 13 August 2025 (reported via press; primary text still to be fetched) requires an exact match of name, gender and date of birth for direct Aadhaar seeding and routes any mismatch through the overhauled Joint Declaration path. The bank- and PAN-side routing remains practitioner-tier. ImprovementPartly done: the Aadhaar seeding and JD gate is anchored to the EPFO circular of 13 August 2025 (fetching the primary text would complete it). Still open: a circular-grade source for the bank-name and PAN-side routing. The re-seed step (corrected details plus document upload) is now modelled.
Claim Filing Two-step form that submits regardless of any KYC mismatch, exactly like the real site. ProA pre-flight completeness check catches both modeled KYC defects (bank-name and father's-name mismatch) before submission, citing the office's own 84% historical rejection rate - and now genuinely closes the loop: "Fix KYC first" detects whichever field is still open and the warning clears automatically once it's fixed, while "File anyway" names only the defect still on file rather than a static claim. ConThe completeness check is scoped to exactly the two defects this persona models. Other real defect types surfaced by the same research (PAN not linked, DOE missing, old UAN issues) aren't checked here. ImprovementDone: the pre-flight now runs off a reusable KYC-field check registry (one entry per defect drives the list item, the warning and the file-anyway text), so adding a newly modeled defect is one registry entry plus its list item, not screen-logic surgery.
Track Claim Status A list plus one opaque remark for the rejected claim: "CLAIM REJECTED DUE TO :- 1) NAME NOT MATCHED WITH BANK RECORDS 2) OTHERS." ProDoes the most work of any screen in the study: a field-by-field rejection decoder, an intent-gated dispute path with a pre-filled EPFiGMS draft, a "verified closure" check that cross-references EPFO's own payment record when a member reports non-payment, and a stalled-claim view against the office's real service standard, now shown as the actual three-tier clock (7 working days aspirational, 20 days with Para 72(7)'s personal-liability and 12% penal-interest consequence, 30 days statutory) instead of one flat number. The OTHERS code now decodes to a real, independently reported defect (a father's-name mismatch on PAN), and a top-of-screen note flags a real, reported false-positive failure mode, both grounded in actual member complaints. Claims now open one at a time as a proper accordion (active row highlighted, View becomes Hide, closing one before opening another), and every illustrative figure on the screen, plus its duplicate on Claim Filing, carries its own inline "(illustrative)" tag rather than one disclaimer stated once. Its per-claim dispute path now also cross-links, in one step, into the standalone Grievance screen with the composer pre-filled from that claim (claim ID, rejection codes, filed and rejected dates), rather than holding a parallel grievance surface of its own. ConThe rejected claim's panel is leaner now that the comparison tables sit behind a disclosure, but it still stacks a decode, a four-item cure list and a collapsed dispute path in one panel. ImprovementDone: the two name-comparison tables now sit behind a "Show the evidence" disclosure, the same pattern as the dispute block, so an opened claim leads with the decode and cure path instead of two tables.
Grievance Redressal No in-portal grievance flow at all; on the real site grievance redressal lives on a separate system (EPFiGMS) the member has to find and navigate on their own. ProA first-class, trackable grievance screen for something that today lives only on a separate portal. A plain-language symptom picker routes each problem deterministically to the right EPFO office and section (no adaptive scoring, no raw department tree); a structured composer captures what happened, the rule or entitlement that applies, and the outcome sought, with a completeness-and-evidence check that blocks submit with a specific correction rather than a generic error. The filed grievance shows a live 21-day reply clock and a status timeline, and once the clock lapses a pre-written, reference-numbered reminder unlocks. On closure it asks the member plainly whether it was actually resolved (Yes / No / Partly); a No or Partly answer reveals a pre-filled appeal (original grievance and closure note verbatim, rule cited, outcome sought) with the 30-day appeal clock and an escalation ladder - EPFiGMS, then CPGRAMS with its 21-day norm, then RTI or the relevant ombudsman - tilted toward a forum independent of the closing office, so a false closure surfaces the appeal path instead of being passively accepted. The ladder now also names EPFO's own internal escalation (7 working days unredressed routes to the Head Office Public Grievance Officer, per the Citizens' Charter) and its 24x7 toll-free helpline, so the member sees who is accountable even before appealing. Track Claim Status's rejected and stalled claims cross-link straight into it, pre-filled. ConNothing is actually filed: the reference number, the office routing, the closure note and the escalation forums are illustrative demo state, not a live EPFiGMS, CPGRAMS or RTI submission. The 21-day reply norm and the 30-day appeal window themselves are now anchored to the DARPG guidelines (Aug 2024) and the CPGRAMS portal FAQ, but the clock still runs from a fixed illustrative "today" that a demo control advances, not the real date, and the routing table maps five symptoms to one office's sections, where a real deployment spans many offices and grievance categories. ImprovementWire the flow to the real EPFiGMS and CPGRAMS systems so a submission, its reference number and its reply clock reflect an actual filing, and broaden the symptom-to-office routing to EPFO's full office and category list. The 21-day reply norm and the 30-day appeal window are now anchored (DARPG Aug 2024 guidelines; CPGRAMS FAQ), and the screen states EPFO's own Citizens' Charter commitment of 7 working days; what remains is anchoring the escalation ladder's RTI and ombudsman rungs to their governing rules.

Passbook Monthly EE / ER / pension contribution table and balances. Modify Basic Details Three-field Aadhaar-match table, "nothing to change" state. ProA "Simulate a Date of Birth mismatch" demo now walks through the actual Joint Declaration routing end to end: the field flips to MISMATCH and a status timeline shows the request going to the employer for JD approval, then to the field office, the real EPFO mechanism this screen exists to explain. ConThe simulation still demonstrates one field, and now three outcomes (routed, approved end to end, or stalled); only the rejected JD branch remains unmodeled. ImprovementDone for approval and stall: "Advance: employer approves" completes the timeline end to end, and "Advance: employer hasn't acted" shows the stalled outcome, matching the stalled-claim pattern on Track Claim Status. Still open: a rejected JD branch (employer disputes the change).
Contact Details Registered mobile and email, OTP-gated change buttons. CosmeticVisual redesign only, otherwise identical. ConNo functional or informational improvement. ImprovementLow priority; the OTP-gated change flow already works the same in both versions. Worth revisiting only if the Alert Centre pattern ever needs to flag a stale or unverified contact detail.
E-Nomination Family declaration table; nomination saved but never e-Signed, modeled identically in both versions. ProAdds an in-screen banner mirroring the Home dashboard's proactive nudge (E-Nomination saved but not e-Signed), with a Complete e-Sign button that scrolls to and focuses the actual e-Sign button below it, so a member who reaches this screen without going through Home still sees the same prompt. ConNothing left to fix here: the banner's button now triggers e-Sign directly, scrolling to and firing the same action the real button would. ImprovementDone: the banner triggers e-Sign directly (the real e-Sign action is already a demo stub in both versions, so nothing more was needed to wire this up).
Mark Exit Exit form plus a rule note: self-marking is only allowed after two months without contribution. ProThe exact eligible date (20-05-2026, two months after the last credit) and the employer's actual update date (25-05-2026) are now shown directly on this screen, not just described as a concept - a member who lands on Mark Exit without going through Home first gets the same benefit. Death Claim · Pension Family/nominee pension filing form (Form-10D). ProSame treatment as the PF death claim: a plain-language panel explaining who typically receives family pension under EPS-95, plus format checks and a not-future date-of-death check on Submit, each with a specific inline correction, and now a soft shape check on the UAN-or-PPO field (a non-conforming entry warns once and passes on re-submit). Reading this honestly

  • 17 of 25 screens carry real weight. Home, Profile, KYC, Claim Filing, Track Claim Status, Grievance Redressal, Passbook, Modify Basic Details, Mark Exit, Transfer Request, Track Application Status, Forgot Password, Activate UAN, Direct UAN Allotment, both Death Claim forms (PF and Pension), and E-Nomination change what the member can see or do. The other 8 change only how things look.
  • The illustrative statistics are a real dependency. The 84% rejection rate and the 34% dispute-reversal rate are invented for this study, explicitly labelled illustrative, and consistent with each other - but the entire persuasive weight of Claim Filing and Track Claim Status rests on real EPFO data eventually matching that shape.
  • Most "fixes" that could be demonstrated in the browser now are. KYC's re-verify actions, Claim Filing's "Fix KYC first" detection, Passbook's Member ID and Financial Year filters, Modify Basic Details' Joint Declaration simulation, Track Application Status's status decoder, Forgot Password's confirmation, the Grievance screen's symptom routing, 21-day reply clock, lapsed-clock reminder, verified-closure question and pre-filled appeal, and both Death Claim forms' validation all genuinely change state end to end, verified in the browser. What's still a promise: the false-positive risk flagged on Track Claim Status is explicitly not something this study can fix, since closing it would require a real backend to connect to; and the Grievance flow files nothing for real (no live EPFiGMS, CPGRAMS or RTI submission), for the same reason.
  • The cosmetic-only screens aren't a failure of scope - they're evidence of consistency. A redesign that only touched the screens with a story to tell would look patched together. Applying one visual language everywhere, even where nothing else changed, is what makes Home, KYC, and Track read as one coherent product instead of a demo bolted onto the old site.
